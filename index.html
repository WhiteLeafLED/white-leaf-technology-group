Technology · Wavelengths

Where LED light sits on the spectrum.

Light is a wave. Two simple measures tell you what kind of light it is.

Frequency

How often it repeats.

Frequency is how many wave cycles pass a point each second. Higher frequency means the wave oscillates faster.

Wavelength

How long each wave is.

Wavelength is the distance from one crest to the next. A longer wavelength means the wave is more stretched out.

Higher frequencies have shorter wavelengths. Lower frequencies have longer wavelengths.

White Leaf’s red and blue LED fixtures sit in that visible band, each at its own wavelength and frequency.

Technology · What is an LED

A light emitting diode.

Light emitting diodes (LEDs) are semiconductors that convert electrical energy into light energy.

The color of the emitted light depends on the semiconductor material and its composition.

LEDs are generally classified into three wavelengths:

  • Ultraviolet
  • Visible
  • Infrared

Technology · Emerging technology

From NASA plant research to cell healing.

In 1993, Quantum Devices of Barneveld, WI developed a light-emitting diode for NASA to use in its plant growth experiments.

A NASA-sponsored research center at the University of Wisconsin-Madison developed Astro culture 3 — a plant growth chamber using near infrared HEALS technology for plant growth experiments on shuttle missions.

LEDs release energy in the form of photons. Their long wavelengths of light stimulate cells to aid in healing — and NASA went on to study LEDs to increase the metabolism of human cells and stem the loss of bone and muscle in astronauts.

In space

Light for astronauts.

NASA’s research extended from plants to people — using LED light to support human cells, bone and muscle on missions.

Human eyes

Light therapy used on human eyes.

LED light therapy is used and being researched for certain eye conditions. It works by boosting mitochondrial function in retinal cells and improving blood circulation, which can help slow disease progression and reduce inflammation.

  • Dry eye & MGD: red and near-infrared masks gently warm the eyelids, melting blockages so the glands can secrete natural oils and stabilize tears.
  • Dry AMD: systems like the Valeda Light Delivery System stimulate energy production in retinal cells, reducing drusen buildup and slowing vision loss.
  • Diabetic retinopathy: near-infrared wavelengths help protect photoreceptors and reduce inflammation-related damage (experimental and clinical).
  • Blepharitis: blue or red light is sometimes applied to reduce bacterial load and inflammation along the eyelids.

Hospitals

Blue light treating jaundice.

Blue light is used to treat jaundice in babies. Here’s how it works:

  • Bilirubin buildup. Jaundice happens when a baby’s immature liver cannot process bilirubin (a yellow pigment from broken-down red blood cells) fast enough, causing it to build up in the blood and turn the skin and eyes yellow.
  • Light absorption. The baby is placed under special therapeutic lights — typically in the blue or blue-green spectrum — wearing only a diaper and protective eye patches.
  • Photochemical change. As the blue light penetrates the skin, it triggers a photochemical reaction (photoisomerization). This changes the structure of the water-insoluble bilirubin into water-soluble isomers like lumirubin.
